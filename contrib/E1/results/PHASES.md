# Why the narrow fitting range does not resolve the corrected coefficient

This is a separate experiment, designed and run after the measurements in
`FINDINGS.md`, to explain two observations that it left open at the time:
the corrected code fits a quadratic coefficient of 47.47 bytes per frame
squared over 15 to 60 s against 131.19 over 15 to 120 s, and XLS-R fits a
negative coefficient over the narrow range.

It is posterior analysis. Nothing in it was stated before the runs, and the
code that produced it, the `--phase-peaks` option of `measure.py`, was written
after them. Every figure here is regenerated by `contrib/E1/recompute.py` from
the files in `contrib/E1/raw/measurements-phases/`.

Units: GiB means 1024 cubed bytes and MB means 10 to the sixth. Quadratic
coefficients are bytes per frame squared.

## A first attempt that was withdrawn

The first attempt compared the `VmHWM` recorded after the first encoder block
with the final `VmHWM`, found them equal at 15, 30 and 45 s, and concluded
that a linear front-end term sets the peak below about 68 s, with a slope of
21.9 MB per second of audio.

The 68 s is withdrawn and stays withdrawn. The reading after the first block
came from a forward hook, which fires after that block returns, so it already
included the attention transient of layer 0, and `VmHWM` is a maximum over the
life of the process rather than a sum, so it cannot be decomposed by
subtraction.

The 21.9 was not a wrong value. It was the right value with the wrong unit:
the measured slope is 23.00 MB per second, which is 21.94 MiB per second. The
first attempt labelled a MiB figure as MB. Correcting the label does not
rescue the 68 s, which failed for the reason above.

## The instrument

`measure.py --phase-peaks` reads `VmHWM` and `VmRSS` in a forward pre-hook of
the first encoder block, before any attention has run, and then resets
`VmHWM` through `/proc/self/clear_refs`. The run therefore yields three
readings. `rss_loaded` is the resident set after the weights are loaded and
before any audio work. `hwm_pre_encoder` is the peak reached by the front end,
the pre-encoder and the positional embedding. `hwm_inference` is the peak
reached by the encoder blocks alone.

Adding the instrument changed no output. The `pooled_sha256` of all 12 phase
runs equals that of the corresponding main run.

## One baseline for both phases

The two phases have to be measured above the same constant. Measuring phase
one above `rss_loaded` and phase two above `rss_pre_encoder` makes the curves
incomparable and the crossover between them meaningless.

Both phases are therefore measured above `rss_loaded`. Each curve is then the
peak that its phase reaches above the state before any audio work, and the peak
of the whole pass is the larger of the two. The gap between the two candidate
baselines, `rss_pre_encoder` minus `rss_loaded`, is 0.05 to 0.10 GiB for
PhoneticXeus and 0.16 to 0.21 GiB for XEUS, which is the size of the error the
choice would introduce.

Phase one is linear in the duration, as a front end that walks the waveform
once should be. The slope is 23.00 MB per second of audio for both encoders,
with `r` of 0.999999 for PhoneticXeus and 1.000000 for XEUS, and intercepts of
12.0 MB and 132.5 MB.

Phase two is quadratic. Fitted over 15 to 120 s it gives 122.26 for
PhoneticXeus and 115.13 for XEUS, against the 128 predicted for the four live
tensors of one masked attention call. Fitted over 15 to 60 s it gives 127.59
for PhoneticXeus, which is closer to the prediction than the wide fit, and
191.87 for XEUS, which overshoots it.

The ratio of phase two to phase one rises without turning in both encoders,
from 0.575 to 1.848 for PhoneticXeus and from 0.714 to 1.903 for XEUS. Above
two different baselines the XEUS ratio instead gives 1.42 at 45 s and 1.43 at
60 s, which does turn. The turn is an artefact of the baselines, not a property
of the encoder.

## The crossover

Interpolating the difference between the two curves linearly between the
points where it changes sign gives 53.67 s for PhoneticXeus and 46.85 s for
XEUS. Above two different baselines the same interpolation gives 58.66 s and
54.72 s, so the choice of baseline moves the crossover by about 5 s and 8 s.

Below the crossover the peak is set by a term linear in the duration, and
above it by the quadratic attention transient. A quadratic fitted to the whole
peak over a range that lies mostly below the crossover is therefore fitted to
a curve that is not yet quadratic, which is the explanation offered for 47.47.

## What this explains, and what it does not

It explains the direction. Over 15 to 60 s the peak of the corrected code is
set by the linear phase at three of the four points, so a quadratic fitted to
it recovers a coefficient far below 128, and over 15 to 120 s, where the
quadratic phase dominates at three points of six, the coefficient comes close.

It does not explain the magnitude for both encoders. With the baseline
corrected, the narrow phase-two fit for PhoneticXeus is 127.59, within 0.32 per
cent of the prediction, which is the strongest form of this evidence. But the
same fit for XEUS gives 191.87, which overshoots by half. The explanation is
therefore partial: the two-regime account is supported for PhoneticXeus and
the XEUS narrow fit remains unexplained.

The byte counts of the retained maps in `FINDINGS.md` do not depend on any of
this. They are exact per layer and per duration, and they are the primary
evidence for the change.
